Hiring an Equine Burial Service in Washington: What to Expect

I've written about what it actually costs to rent a backhoe and bury a horse yourself, and about the real legal requirements that rule out most Washington properties in the first place. What I haven't written about directly is the option that sits between those two posts: hiring a horse burial service — someone who brings the equipment, already knows the legal requirements cold, and does the physical work for you. If you're weighing DIY against hiring it out, here's an honest look at what that actually involves.

If your horse died unexpectedly and you haven't decided on a direction yet, start with what to do first instead — this post assumes burial is already the direction you're leaning, and you're deciding whether to do it yourself or hire it out.

What Hiring an Equine Burial Service Actually Means

An equine burial service isn't a different kind of burial than the DIY version — it's the same legal requirements, the same hole, the same three feet of cover. What changes is who does the work. Instead of renting a backhoe yourself, coordinating delivery, confirming your property's acreage and setbacks, and operating equipment you may have never touched before, you're paying someone who already owns the equipment, already knows Washington's requirements, and does this often enough that it isn't a first attempt.

Why Owners Choose to Hire This Out Instead of DIY

  • Heavy equipment access — a backhoe or excavator capable of digging a hole large enough for a horse isn't something most people own, and renting one for a single job usually means also renting a tractor to move the horse, then learning to operate both under pressure, on the worst possible day
  • Knowing the legal requirements cold — WAC 16-25-025's acreage, setback, depth, and land-use requirements are specific enough that getting one wrong turns a legal burial into an illegal one, and I've spent enough time working inside those requirements that I'm not guessing at them job to job
  • Not having to do the physical work while grieving — digging, moving, and burying a 1,000-pound animal is genuinely hard physical labor, and doing that yourself on the same day you lost your horse is a different experience than making one phone call and letting someone else handle it

None of that means DIY is the wrong call for every situation — some owners genuinely want to be the ones who do it, and that's a real, valid choice too. But industry guidance on equine disposal consistently points to the same practical friction: owners need to arrange a backhoe to dig the hole and often a tractor to move the horse into it, on top of confirming local rules first — real logistics that don't disappear just because you're doing it yourself.

What I Actually Do When You Call

When someone calls me about burial rather than removal, the first thing I do is talk through whether their property actually qualifies — most don't, and I'd rather tell you that honestly up front than take a job I can't legally do. If it does qualify, I bring the equipment, dig to the depth and location Washington's rule requires, and handle the labor start to finish, so the only thing left for you to do is decide it's what you want.

What It Costs Compared to DIY

I don't publish a flat rate for this, the same way I don't for removal — every property is different, and access, soil, and distance all affect the job. But it's worth weighing against the real DIY equipment numbers I've written up elsewhere: a compact backhoe rental with delivery commonly runs $700 to $1,100 for a single day, before you've done any of the actual digging yourself. Hiring it out replaces that rental cost, your own time, and the physical and legal risk of doing it without experience, with one number and someone else doing the work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is hiring a burial service different from hiring removal? Yes — removal means the horse leaves your property; burial means I bring equipment and bury the horse on your land, provided it meets Washington's legal requirements.

Do I still need to confirm my property qualifies if I hire this out? Yes — the legal requirements under WAC 16-25-025 apply no matter who does the digging. I'll help confirm this honestly before taking the job.

Is hiring a burial service more expensive than doing it myself? Not necessarily once you count equipment rental, your own time, and the risk of a mistake — reach out for a real number for your situation.

What if my property doesn't qualify for burial at all? Then burial isn't a legal option regardless of who does the digging — cremation or removal are the realistic paths, and I'll walk you through both honestly.
