How Much Does It Cost to Cremate a Horse in Washington?

I get asked about cremation cost almost as often as I get asked about removal itself, even though it isn't something I do. I'd rather give you an honest, real answer than dodge the question just because it's not my service — so here's what horse cremation actually costs, what drives the price, and how it stacks up against your other real options.

Private vs. Communal Cremation

Private cremation means your horse is cremated alone, and the ashes are returned to you, usually in a container or urn. Communal cremation means multiple animals are cremated together, and no ashes come back to you. Private is the more expensive option nearly everywhere, simply because it takes dedicated equipment time for one animal instead of sharing a cremation run with others.

What It Actually Costs

Across multiple providers, whole-horse cremation typically runs somewhere between $600 and $2,000, with a lot of that spread coming down to private versus communal and how far the horse has to be transported. One real Washington-based provider, PALSNW in Washougal, publishes weight-based pricing: $2.25 per pound if they pick up and transport your horse, or $1.75 per pound if you transport the horse to them yourself (their standard service area is within 100 miles of Washougal, with additional mileage billed beyond that). For a horse in the common 1,000–1,200 pound range, that works out to roughly $2,250–$2,700 with pickup, or $1,750–$2,100 self-transported — real numbers, not a rough guess, though every provider prices differently and minimums can apply.

What Drives the Price Up or Down

  • Your horse's weight — bigger horses need more fuel and cremation time, and draft horses often carry a surcharge
  • Private vs. communal — private costs more because it isn't shared with other animals
  • Transport distance — pickup fees and mileage add up quickly outside a provider's base service area
  • Extras — upgraded urns, witnessed cremation, or expedited return of ashes all add to the total
  • Timing — after-hours, weekend, or holiday service can carry an added fee

How This Compares to Your Other Real Options

  • Legal on-property burial — only an option if your land genuinely meets Washington's acreage and setback requirements; see what that actually costs if it does
  • Rendering — rarely available, and usually not an option at all if the animal was euthanized, which I explain in what happens after a horse is euthanized
  • Professional removal and transport — what I do, coordinated directly with your vet's schedule; I don't publish a flat rate since it depends on location and access, but a real quote takes one call

Why I Don't Do Cremation Myself

Cremation is a genuinely different business — it takes dedicated equipment I don't run, and I'd rather be straightforward about that than pretend otherwise. What I focus on is prompt, respectful removal and transport, often right at the time a horse is euthanized or has just passed. If cremation is what you actually want, I'll tell you that honestly and point you toward it rather than trying to talk you into a service that isn't the right fit for what you're looking for.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to cremate a horse? Typically $600 to $2,000 or more, depending on whether you choose private or communal cremation, your horse's weight, and how far it needs to be transported. Weight-based pricing from a real Washington provider runs roughly $1.75 to $2.25 per pound.

Is communal cremation cheaper than private? Yes, noticeably — communal cremation shares equipment time across multiple animals and doesn't return ashes, which is why it lands toward the lower end of the price range.

Do you offer horse cremation? No — I focus on removal and transport. I'll always tell you honestly if cremation is the better fit for your situation, even though it isn't a service I provide myself.
