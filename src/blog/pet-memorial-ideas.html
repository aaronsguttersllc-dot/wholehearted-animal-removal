Pet Memorial Ideas: Real Ways to Remember a Pet

Most of what I write about is the logistics — what's legal, what things cost, who to call. This post is different. Once the immediate decisions are handled, a lot of people want a real way to remember their pet, not just a completed task. Here's an honest rundown of what's actually out there.

Keepsakes, If You Chose Cremation

If your pet was cremated, there's a real range of ways to keep something physical. Cremation jewelry — a small pendant filled with a portion of the ashes — is a genuine, established option; providers like Spirit Pieces mail a free collection kit and need only about a quarter teaspoon of ash to make a piece, with roughly a six-week turnaround. Paw print or nose print kits are another common option, either done yourself or offered by some cremation providers as part of their aftercare. If your pet was buried rather than cremated, fur clipped beforehand is a real alternative for a keepsake locket or container.

Memorial Gardens and Planting

Planting something over or near a burial site is common, informal practice rather than a formal program — there's no single authority governing it. If you're planning to plant directly over a grave, shallow-rooted perennials or shrubs are generally the safer choice over a tree, since deep root systems can be a practical complication over time. The ASPCA maintains a plant database that's useful for checking whether something you're considering is safe if you have other pets who'll be around the garden.

Grave Markers and Headstones

If you buried your pet and want a permanent marker, real pricing ranges widely: simple slate or resin garden markers start around $39 to $115, cast stone or cultured marble options from around $88, and granite markers from roughly $349, with fully custom granite work running anywhere from about $80 to $2,500 or more depending on size and detail. A simple wooden cross is a common low-cost, DIY option, though I couldn't find a dedicated commercial source with real pricing for that specifically — treat it as a well-known practice rather than a priced product.

Digital Memorials

Online memorial pages are a real, low-cost option if you want something shareable. Ever Loved is a genuine, active service offering free pet tribute pages. I found several other pet-memorial sites mentioned online that I wasn't able to independently confirm are still active, so I'd rather point you to the one I could verify than list names I can't stand behind.

Washington Pet Cemeteries

Chambers Creek Pet Memorial Park & Crematory in University Place (part of New Tacoma Cemeteries) offers individual cremation, home or vet pickup, burial, urns, and grave markers, and directly serves the Pierce County area. There's also Seattle-Tacoma Pet Cemetery in Kent — a real, historic cemetery dating to around 1950 and a King County Landmark — but I want to be straightforward about something: Seattle Times reporting from late 2024 into early 2025 found it in real disrepair, with maintenance and management disputes and at least one owner reporting an unfulfilled paid headstone. Zoning issues were corrected in late 2024, but given that reporting, I'd treat it with real caution rather than a clean recommendation until you've done your own current-day check on its condition.

Real Grief Support, If You Want to Talk to Someone

  • Tufts Cummings School of Veterinary Medicine Pet Loss Support Helpline — (508) 839-7966, staffed evenings with 24-hour voicemail
  • Cornell University Pet Loss Support Hotline — (607) 218-7457, staffed by trained veterinary student volunteers
  • Losing a pet is real grief — reaching out to a dedicated hotline isn't an overreaction, it's what these lines exist for

Frequently Asked Questions

What's a reasonably priced way to memorialize a pet? A slate or resin grave marker (roughly $39-$115) or a free digital memorial page through a service like Ever Loved are both real, low-cost options.

Is cremation jewelry a real thing, or just a marketing gimmick? It's genuine — established providers like Spirit Pieces make real jewelry from a small portion of actual ashes, typically needing only about a quarter teaspoon.

Are there real pet cemeteries near Tacoma? Chambers Creek Pet Memorial Park in University Place is a real, currently operating option. Seattle-Tacoma Pet Cemetery in Kent is real and historic, but recent reporting found it in disrepair — worth checking its current condition yourself before committing.

Is it normal to want grief support after losing a pet? Completely. Dedicated pet loss hotlines exist at Tufts and Cornell specifically because this kind of grief is real and common.
